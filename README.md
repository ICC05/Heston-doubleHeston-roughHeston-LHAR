# Heston, double Heston and rough Heston — indirect inference estimation with the LHAR auxiliary criterion

MATLAB code for the **indirect inference** (EMSM) estimation of three stochastic volatility models on 5-minute returns of the SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY), using the **LHAR** model of Corsi and Renò (2012) as the auxiliary criterion.

The code accompanies the research project *Indirect inference estimation of the rough Heston model* (I. Cardosi Carrara, Sant'Anna School of Advanced Studies, 2026), Sections 5 and 7.2 of the project report.

---

## 1. The idea: indirect inference

Continuous-time stochastic volatility models (Heston, double Heston, rough Heston) are easy to **simulate**, but their likelihood is either unavailable in closed form or too expensive to evaluate. Indirect inference works around this:

1. choose a simple **auxiliary model**, estimable by OLS, that summarizes the features of the data the structural model should reproduce;
2. estimate it on the **observed data** → empirical moment vector β̂;
3. for each candidate θ of structural parameters, **simulate** many paths, rebuild realized variance, re-estimate the auxiliary model on each path and average → simulated *binding function* b̂(θ);
4. choose θ to minimize the weighted quadratic distance

   χ²(θ) = [β̂ − b̂(θ)]ᵀ Ω [β̂ − b̂(θ)],  where Ω is the inverse covariance matrix of the empirical moments.

The random shocks are held **fixed** across candidate θ (common random numbers), so that changes in χ² reflect θ rather than Monte Carlo noise.

## 2. Data

- SPY intraday transaction prices, January 2, 1990 – December 31, 2013.
- 5-minute log-returns → **daily realized variance** RVₜ = Σ r²ₜ,ᵢ (overnight returns excluded).
- Cleaning:
  - low-activity days removed (>50% zero returns);
  - the most extreme 0.5% of locally standardized returns discarded;
  - August–December 2008 window excluded.
- Final sample: **T = 5,867 days**, stored in `spy_data.mat`.

## 3. Auxiliary criterion: LHAR

The LHAR model extends the HAR-RV of Corsi (2009) with leverage terms:

log RVₜ₊₁ = α + β⁽ᵈ⁾ log RVₜ⁽ᵈ⁾ + β⁽ʷ⁾ log RVₜ⁽ʷ⁾ + β⁽ᵐ⁾ log RVₜ⁽ᵐ⁾ + γ⁽ᵈ⁾ rₜ⁽ᵈ⁾⁻ + γ⁽ʷ⁾ rₜ⁽ʷ⁾⁻ + γ⁽ᵐ⁾ rₜ⁽ᵐ⁾⁻ + εₜ₊₁

- the β's capture **heterogeneous persistence** (daily, weekly = 5 days, monthly = 22 days);
- the γ's capture the **leverage effect** (past negative returns → higher future volatility).

The **q = 8 auxiliary moments** are the 7 coefficients plus the residual variance. The weighting matrix is the inverse of the Newey-West HAC covariance (Bartlett kernel, 44 lags). The residual variance is treated as an orthogonal block with variance 2σ̂⁴/n.

## 4. Structural models

| Model | Parameters | p | Identification (q = 8) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heston** (1 factor) | κ, v̄, σᵥ, ρ | 4 | over-identified (4 restrictions) |
| **Double Heston** (2 factors, Christoffersen et al. 2010) | κ₁, κ₂, v̄₁, v̄₂, σ₁, σ₂, ρ₁, ρ₂ | 8 | just-identified (q = p) |
| **Rough Heston** (El Euch and Rosenbaum 2019) | λ, θ, ν, ρ, H | 5 | over-identified (3 restrictions) |

### Simulation
- **Heston / double Heston**:
  - intraday grid of M = 80 steps per day, N = 6,000 simulated days;
  - the CIR variance is propagated with the *exact conditional-moment-matching* scheme of Corsi and Renò (2012), with full-truncation positivity correction;
  - returns are generated by Euler-Maruyama.
- **Rough Heston**:
  - the singular fractional kernel (t−s)^(α−1)/Γ(α) is approximated by a **sum of N = 20 exponentials** (multifactor Markovian approximation of Abi Jaber and El Euch, 2019);
  - nodes lie on a geometric grid between 0.1 and 100 (1/day); weights come from non-negative L² least squares, are pre-tabulated on a grid of H and interpolated cubically;
  - stability requires a finer grid: M = 400 steps per day, later aggregated to 80 five-minute intervals;
  - since the process is non-Markovian, a **1,000-day burn-in** is discarded.

### Variance reduction
- **H = 494 effective replications**: 247 independent shock streams plus their antithetic versions.
- Shocks are not stored but regenerated on the fly from 247 fixed Threefry seeds.
- Replications are evaluated in parallel (`parfor`).

### Optimization (two stages)
1. **CMA-ES** (Hansen and Ostermeier, 2001):
   - population λ = 20, up to 300 generations;
   - 10 restarts with Latin Hypercube initial means;
   - restarts with χ² ≥ 5× the best are relaunched from an antithetic point (at most 3 relaunches).
2. **Bound-constrained pattern search** (Audet and Dennis, 2002), from 5 starting points around the CMA-ES optimum.

### Validation diagnostics
- **Out-of-sample χ²**: the optimum is re-evaluated under independent seeds. If the OOS χ² is much higher than the in-sample one, the optimizer has fitted Monte Carlo noise rather than the mapping from parameters to moments.
- **Monte Carlo noise floor**: χ² at the optimum over 10 different seeds, and signal-to-noise ratio χ²_min / std(χ²).
- **Cross-restart dispersion** of the most delicate parameter (ρ, ρ₁/ρ₂ or H).
- **Per-moment** decomposition of χ².

## 5. Results (from the project report)

| Model | In-sample χ² | Out-of-sample χ² | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heston | 719.96 | 723.10 | Fails. It concentrates persistence at the daily horizon (β⁽ᵈ⁾ = 0.79 vs 0.25) and generates no leverage (γ ≈ 0); κ and v̄ hit their bounds. |
| Double Heston | 7.03 | 174.63 | Reproduces almost all LHAR moments, through a fast and a slow factor and the leverage "overshooting" mechanism. However, it is just-identified and the OOS χ² explodes: the fit is partly mechanical. |
| Rough Heston | 701.25 | 705.83 | Fails like the Heston and even gets the leverage sign wrong (ρ̂ = +0.38). The LHAR contains no statistic sensitive to roughness (H). |

**Takeaway**: a single variance factor, Markovian or rough, cannot reproduce the LHAR stylized facts. The double Heston can, but with q = p the result is not conclusive. This motivates the **RAUX** criterion introduced in the project and implemented in the [roughHeston-RAUX](https://github.com/ICC05/roughHeston-RAUX) and [doubleHeston-RAUX](https://github.com/ICC05/doubleHeston-RAUX) repositories.

All claims concern the **reproduction** of the auxiliary moments, not the **identification** of the individual structural parameters. The latter would require profile likelihood and parametric bootstrap, which are left for future work.

## 6. How to run

Requirements: base MATLAB (no mandatory toolbox) plus Parallel Computing Toolbox (recommended; without it the code runs serially but very slowly).

From the repository folder, run in MATLAB:

```matlab
estimate_heston_LHAR_new2          % Heston
estimate_double_heston_LHAR_new2   % Double Heston
estimate_rough_heston_LHAR_new2    % Rough Heston
```

Each script writes to the current folder:
- `results_<model>_LHAR_new2.mat`: struct `est` with parameters, χ², simulated moments and diagnostics;
- `results_<model>_LHAR_new2.txt`: results table;
- `logfile_<model>_LHAR_new2.txt`: full run log.

The code is designed for many-core machines: the number of replications is tuned for a 247-worker pool, and on a standard PC each estimation can take days. To reduce run time, lower `cfg.n_repl` in `setup_data_new.m` and `cfg.n_indep` in `setup_data_rough_new.m`, at the cost of more Monte Carlo noise.

A description of every file is in [`CODE.md`](CODE.md).
